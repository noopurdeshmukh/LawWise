THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 — KEY PROVISIONS
Source: Legislative Department, Ministry of Law and Justice, Government of India
Jurisdiction: India
Document Type: Act / Statute
Year: 1872 (with amendments)

================================================================
OVERVIEW
================================================================

The Indian Contract Act, 1872 is the principal legislation governing contracts in India. It defines the conditions under which promises made by parties to a contract are legally binding.

================================================================
ESSENTIAL ELEMENTS OF A VALID CONTRACT (Section 10)
================================================================

All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void.

For a contract to be valid, it must contain:
1. Offer and Acceptance
2. Free Consent (Section 14)
3. Competency of Parties (Section 11)
4. Lawful Consideration (Section 2(d))
5. Lawful Object (Section 23)
6. Certainty of Terms (Section 29)
7. Possibility of Performance (Section 56)

================================================================
FREE CONSENT (Sections 13-22)
================================================================

Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense (Section 13).

Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by:
- Coercion (Section 15): committing or threatening to commit any act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code
- Undue influence (Section 16): dominating the will of another to obtain unfair advantage
- Fraud (Section 17): active concealment of a fact or false representation
- Misrepresentation (Section 18): positive assertion of what is not true
- Mistake (Sections 20-22): both parties are under a mistake as to a matter of fact

================================================================
CONSIDERATION (Section 2(d))
================================================================

When, at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person has done or abstained from doing, or does or abstains from doing, or promises to do or to abstain from doing, something, such act or abstinence or promise is called a consideration for the promise.

A contract without consideration is void (Section 25), except in cases:
- Natural love and affection between close relatives (written and registered)
- Compensation for past voluntary services
- Promise to pay a time-barred debt (written)

================================================================
BREACH OF CONTRACT AND REMEDIES
================================================================

When a party to a contract breaks the contract (Section 39), the other party may:

1. Rescind the contract (Section 39)
2. Sue for damages (Sections 73-75)
3. Sue for specific performance
4. Seek injunction

Compensation for loss or damage (Section 73):
When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken it, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it.

Remote and indirect loss (Section 73):
Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach.

Duty to mitigate (Section 73):
The aggrieved party has a duty to mitigate the losses arising from breach of contract. Failure to do so may reduce the compensation awarded.

================================================================
INDEMNITY AND GUARANTEE (Sections 124-147)
================================================================

Contract of Indemnity (Section 124):
A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person, is called a "contract of indemnity."

Rights of Indemnity-holder when sued (Section 125):
The promisee in a contract of indemnity, acting within the scope of his authority, is entitled to recover from the promisor:
(1) all damages which he may be compelled to pay in any suit in respect of any matter to which the promise to indemnify applies
(2) all costs which he may be compelled to pay in any such suit
(3) all sums which he may have paid under the terms of any compromise of any such suit

Contract of Guarantee (Section 126):
A "contract of guarantee" is a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default. The person who gives the guarantee is called the "surety"; the person in respect of whose default the guarantee is given is called the "principal debtor", and the person to whom the guarantee is given is called the "creditor."

================================================================
AGENCY (Sections 182-238)
================================================================

Agent and Principal (Section 182):
An "agent" is a person employed to do any act for another, or to represent another in dealings with third persons. The person for whom such act is done, or who is so represented, is called the "principal."

Extent of agent's authority (Section 188):
An agent having an authority to do an act has authority to do every lawful thing which is necessary in order to do such act.

Liability of principal for acts of agent (Section 226):
Contracts entered into through an agent, and obligations arising from acts done by an agent, may be enforced in the same manner, and will have the same legal consequences, as if the contracts had been entered into and the acts done by the principal in person.

================================================================
SPECIFIC RELIEF
================================================================

Specific performance of a contract may be granted when:
- Compensation in money would not afford adequate relief
- The act agreed to be done is such that breach thereof cannot be adequately relieved by compensation
- It would be extremely difficult to ascertain the actual damage caused by non-performance
